How to Play

The book way: read on, roll your d20, and let the Hat's Whisper referee — everything you need is on this page.

The game-master way: play it as a living conversation at The Table — the first tab. Write what Remus does; the Game Master improvises the scene, voices the cast, calls for dice when the moment earns them, and keeps the Case File. The Game Master runs on an AI service with a key of your own; the meter in the top bar shows what each turn cost.

What you need: a d20 (one is built into The Table, beside the chat, along with your character sheet — which saves itself in this browser). A full dice set is welcome but optional: the book finds honest work for a d4 (duel bruises), d8 and d12 (your wand), d10s (exams), and the rest of the family (tables of chance) — every such roll also tells you how to fake it with the d20 alone. Nothing else. Rules first, then the story. If you'd rather begin buying your school things immediately, read The Rules of Magic and Who You Are, then go straight to Chapter I.

I. The Rules of Magic

Everything you attempt that might fail — coaxing a spell, charming a prefect, creeping past a sleeping portrait — is a Check. Roll the d20, add one of your six Stats, and compare the total to a Difficulty (DC). You may roll the built-in die, or roll your own d20 at your desk and use the enter a roll field in the dice tool so the book can mark triumphs and disasters for you.

DCHow it feelsExample
8SimpleLevitating a feather in an empty classroom
12TrickyLevitating it while Peeves blows raspberries at you
16HardLevitating your friend's dropped wand back to her mid-duel
20The stuff of school legendLevitating a full-grown troll's club mid-swing. Someone managed it once, allegedly, in the 1830s. The portraits still argue about it.

Reading the Die

  • Beat the DC by 5 or more — a flourish: you succeed with style. Take a small extra benefit (you choose, within reason).
  • Natural 20 — a triumph. Succeed magnificently; if anyone is watching, award yourself +1 House Point.
  • Natural 1 — a calamity. You fail and something complicates. If you were casting a spell, roll on the Table of Miscasts.
  • Edge / Burden — when circumstance favors you (a friend helping, the right tool, good preparation) roll twice and keep the better die. When it doesn't (darkness, fear, a rival's sabotage), roll twice and keep the worse. They cancel one-for-one.

Luck

You hold a pool of Luck points equal to 2 + your Luck stat, refreshed at the start of every chapter. Spend one to: reroll any d20 you just rolled (keep the second result), or declare a small stroke of fortune — the staircase happens to swing your way, you did pack a spare quill, the caretaker sneezes at the perfect moment. Be modest; the castle notices greed.

Stamina

Your Stamina is 8 + Nerve. Miscasts, falls, frights and duels drain it. At 0 you are carried to the Hospital Wing: skip your next Downtime activity, wake with full Stamina and one fewer point of dignity. You can never die in this game — but you can owe Madam Bracken a very awkward explanation.

Duelling

When wands are drawn, fight in exchanges. Your foe has a Form (a DC) and Hits (how many touches defeat them). Each exchange:

  • Roll d20 + Magic against their Form. Succeed: your spell lands — they lose 1 Hit. Fail: theirs lands — you lose 1d4 Stamina (roll a real d4 if you own one; with only the d20: 1–5 means 1, 6–10 means 2, 11–15 means 3, 16–20 means 4).
  • A triumph costs them 2 Hits; a calamity means a miscast in front of everyone.
  • Naming a fitting spell you've learned (Flipendo to knock, Expelliarmus to disarm) gives you Edge on that exchange, once per spell per duel.

The Ledger of Points & Marks

Track three slow currencies on your sheet:

  • House Points — your personal contribution, won in lessons and lost in corridors. They matter in Chapter IX.
  • Study — each class (Charms, Transfiguration, Potions, Defence, Herbology, Astronomy, History of Magic) has a Study score from 0 to +5, raised in lessons and Downtime. Exams are rolled with it.
  • Bonds — each friend has a Bond from 0 to 5, and it climbs slowly: one heart is a saved seat, two is everything shared, three is a rule broken for you, four is into the dark beside you, and five — kindred — is a year's worth of showing up. Raise them by choosing people over convenience. At Bond 2+ a friend will break rules for you; at 3 they'd follow you into the dark — and in Chapter IX, one of them may have to.
  • Marks — story flags written like ☐ The Brass Weight. When the text says take the Mark, tick it in your satchel notes. Later scenes ask whether you carry it.

Money

Wizard money is Galleons (G) and Sickles (s), 17 Sickles to the Galleon. You begin Chapter I with your school fund: 80 Galleons, and every Galleon spent is a choice.

The Pregenerated Hero (play him, or build your own above)

Dylan Mooney, eleven, of Croydon and two worlds. His mother is a witch — Slytherin in her day, with stories about a common room under a lake that he half thought were bedtime invention until July. His father is a Muggle firefighter, which Dylan privately considers the braver kind of magic. A younger sister stays behind in the Muggle world, unconvinced the whole thing isn't an elaborate prank. Dylan wears his Converse with his school robes and intends to keep doing so until specifically stopped by law.

V. Cauldron & Ladle

Brewing is slow spellwork done with your sleeves rolled up. A potion is a sequence of three Wits checks — Preparation (chop, crush, measure), the Delicate Moment (the potion's own DC), and the Finish (stir pattern and patience, DC 10). Fail one: the batch is Dubious (half effect, odd side-effect of your choosing). Fail two: it's Ruined, and roll the Twist. Your toad, if you have one, lets you reroll one Preparation check per brew.

Chapter I

Diagon Alley

The last day of August, and London doesn't know. Buses grind past a grubby little pub that no one on the pavement can see — no one except you, clutching a letter with your name on it in emerald ink.

Scene 1 · The Wall That Counts

Inside the Leaky Cauldron it is all pipe-smoke and low beams and a barman who calls you "first-year, are we?" before you've said a word. Whoever brought you here — decide who that is: a parent, your uncle, a brisk Ministry escort for muggle-borns — leads you to a brick courtyard that dead-ends at a wall. Three up, two across. The bricks shiver, fold, and open into a street that shouldn't fit inside London, all crooked gables and gold-lettered signs and owls arguing overhead.

Insight, DC 10. On a success: among the crowd you notice a man in a grey travelling cloak, hood up despite the warmth, who is not shopping. He is watching the entrances of shops — and, for one skipped heartbeat, watching you. Take the Mark ☐ Saw the Grey Cloak. On a failure, you notice only the smell of toffee and owl feathers, which is fair; it's a lot.

Scene 2 · Gringotts

A snow-white building leaning over the street like a headmaster over homework. The doors are burnished bronze, and the warning engraved on the silver ones inside rhymes, which somehow makes it worse. A goblin teller named Vekk looks at you over half-moon spectacles with the expression of someone who has seen ten thousand first-years and been impressed by four.

Vekk: "School fund. Vault nine hundred and forty. You will want" — he weighs you with a glance — "all eighty. They always want all eighty. Mind your elbows in the cart."

The cart. Nerve, DC 8, to keep your breakfast and your dignity through the plunging dark. Flourish: you whoop, and Vekk's mouth does something that on a goblin passes for a smile — Edge on your next Charm check with any goblin, ever. Calamity: you lose nothing but your voice for an hour, and Vekk tells the other tellers.

Record 80 Galleons in your satchel.

Scene 4 · Ollivanders

A narrow shop, dust in the sunbeams, ten thousand slim boxes sleeping on their shelves. The bell has barely stopped when a soft voice comes from the ladder.

Ollivander: "Ah. I wondered when I would be seeing you. Hold out your wand arm. No — your wand arm. Yes. Hmm."

A tape measure measures things that cannot need measuring — the distance between your nostrils, your optimism. Boxes come down. Wands are put in your hand and snatched away half a wave later. Something smashes; he looks delighted. And then one wand warms in your fingers like a held hand, and the dust in the sunbeam swirls slowly around you, and the shop goes quiet.

Roll your wand — d12 wood, d4 core, d8 temperament — and write it in your satchel. Its temperament is a real rule.

Playing Dylan Mooney? Skip the rolls: the wand that warms in his hand is cherry and thestral tail-hair, eleven and a quarter inches, Bold — a show-off's wood on a core that only chooses those who'll one day understand quiet. Ollivander looks from the wand to the boy in the Converse for a long moment and says only: "Curious pairing. Loud shoes, quiet core. Do look after each other." He does not explain, and he does not charge for the polish.

Scene 6 · The Man in Grey

Late gold light. You are juggling parcels outside Flourish & Blotts when a girl with ink on her nose and a tower of secondhand books walks into you, apologizes to the books first, and grins.

Girl: "Sorry! First year? Me too. I'm buying everything. Well — everything used. Did you know the pictures move? Nobody tells you the pictures move. I'm Posy, by the way. Posy Fenwick."

Before you can answer, the crowd folds, and the man in the grey travelling cloak is simply there, stumbling against your cauldron with a murmured pardon, one gloved hand steadying its rim. Then he is gone into the crush by Quality Quidditch Supplies, walking like a man who has remembered an appointment. Posy blinks after him.

Posy: "Rude. He didn't even knock anything over, which is somehow worse?"

Insight, DC 12. Success: there is something new in your cauldron — a brass page-weight the size of a fat coin, cold as a well, stamped with an open book whose pages are blank. It hums very slightly against your fingers, like the memory of a note. Take the Mark ☐ The Brass Weight and write it in your inventory. Failure: you find it tonight instead, tangled in your new gloves, and it is somehow colder. Take the Mark anyway.

If you show it to Posy, she turns it over twice and says, "It's got no maker's stamp. Everything has a maker's stamp — my dad prints beer mats, I know these things." Take the Mark ☐ Posy Saw the Weight.

That night, at the Leaky Cauldron or your own bed, the last thing you hear before sleep is a sound like a single page turning, very far away. Chapter I ends. Refresh your Luck. Total your remaining gold. Sleep does come. Eventually.

Chapter II

The Hogwarts Express

King's Cross, the first of September, five to eleven. Between platforms nine and ten there is a barrier that only looks solid, and on the far side of it the twentieth century gives up entirely.

Scene 1 · The Barrier

Run at the brick. That is genuinely the whole instruction. Nerve, DC 8 — failure means you balk twice and a porter watches you with growing concern before you get through on the third try, arriving on Platform Nine and Three-Quarters flustered but intact. And there it is: the Hogwarts Express, scarlet as a guardsman, breathing steam over a platform full of cats and trunks and mothers straightening collars. An owl escapes somewhere to general applause.

Scene 2 · The Compartment

You haul your trunk down the corridor (Nerve DC 8; on a failure a cheerful seventh-year simply lifts it one-handed, which is worse than any injury). The first compartment with room holds a familiar tower of secondhand books and, behind it, Posy Fenwick — and by the window, a small neat boy with black hair and a folded map on his knees, who looks up, considers you completely, and returns to the map.

Posy: "It's you! Cauldron-crash! Sit down, sit — this is Idris. Idris Bellchant. He's said nine words since Reading and I've counted every one."

Idris: "Ten."

Posy: "Ten. He's from a mapmaking family. Wizard maps. Maps of places that move. Show them the—"

Idris: — folding the map away — "Later, maybe."

Talk with them as long as you like. When the trolley witch knocks — an old, kind voice: "Anything from the trolley, dears?" — pumpkin pasties are 2 knuts, cauldron cakes 3, Chocolate Frogs 2 sickles. Buying a round for the compartment is 5 sickles and raises Posy's Bond to 1 without a roll.

Scene 3 · Rooke

Past the Midlands, the compartment door slides open without a knock. The girl in the doorway has a prefect's poise without the badge, silver-fair plaits pinned like a coronet, and the expression of someone auditing the train.

Girl: "Lucinda Rooke. I'm introducing myself to everyone worth knowing in our year." Her eyes pass over the secondhand books, over Idris's boots, and land on you. "The Rookes have been at Hogwarts for eleven generations. Slytherin, mostly, and a Ravenclaw we don't discuss. And you are—?"

Choose your opening move with her; it matters all year:

  • Be courteous, give your name. Charm, DC 10. Success: a fractional nod — "Acceptable." Take the Mark ☐ Rooke Knows Your Name.
  • Defend the compartment — "Everyone here is worth knowing." Nerve, DC 12. Success: her eyebrows lift a millimetre, which for a Rooke is a gasp; take ☐ Rooke Knows Your Name and Posy's Bond +1. Failure: she smiles like frost forming. Take ☐ Rooke's List — you're on it.
  • Say nothing and hold her gaze. Insight, DC 12. Success: you see, under all that polish, a girl terrified of being ordinary. Take ☐ Saw Rooke Clearly — Edge on all Charm checks with her, forever.

Rooke, leaving: "Well. I expect we'll be seeing a great deal of each other, or nothing at all. Both are fine."

Scene 4 · North

Fields go blue with evening. Somewhere after the mountains begin, Idris speaks without being spoken to, once:

Idris: "You keep touching your pocket. Left one. Since London." He looks at you for the second time in six hours. "Whatever's in it — it's pulling north. Same as us."

If you show him the brass weight (requires the Mark ☐ The Brass Weight): he holds his palm flat above it, not touching, the way you'd check a kettle. "It's a key," he says at last. "Not the kind that opens doors. The kind that opens appetites. My gran had a lock like this on her pantry." He will say nothing else about it tonight. Take the Mark ☐ Idris Saw the Weight and 1 Clue.

The train slows. HOGSMEADE, says the sign, in lamplight, and over the loudspeaker of sheer tradition a voice calls: "Firs' years! Firs' years, this way — mind the step—" A lantern swings above the crowd, held by a barrel-chested man with a beard like a hedge in winter: Ogg, the gamekeeper. Chapter II ends. Refresh Luck.

Chapter III

The Sorting

Four to a boat, and no one rows. The lake is black glass; the cold comes up off it like a held breath. Then the boats round the headland, and the whole year — all of you, even the ones pretending otherwise — goes silent at once.

Scene 1 · Across the Lake

Hogwarts stands on its cliff with every window burning, towers stacked against the stars like a thing grown rather than built. In your boat: Posy (gripping the gunwale, radiant), Idris (making a quiet correction to a map he isn't holding), and one more — decide who, or let it be a round-faced boy named Fen Ap­pleby who will be sick with nerves and grateful forever if you talk him through it (Charm or Insight DC 8; success: Fen's Bond becomes 1 — it never needs to be higher; Fen repays kindness at the strangest moments all year).

Something vast and gentle breaks the surface a boat-length away — a grey tentacle, curling with what can only be called politeness. Posy shakes its tip. This is the best moment of her life so far.

Scene 2 · The Hat

Up stone steps, through doors an ogre couldn't shoulder, into an entrance hall where the ceiling is a rumor. A tall wizard in midnight-blue robes waits with a list — auburn hair, auburn beard, half-moon spectacles over eyes that seem to find each of you individually amusing and precious at once.

Professor Dumbledore: "Welcome. In a moment you will be Sorted, which sounds alarming and is, at worst, itchy. I teach Transfiguration; if tonight you find your teacup looking back at you, do tell it good evening. Follow me."

The Great Hall: four long tables, a thousand candles floating under a ceiling that is tonight's actual sky, and every face turned to your small procession. On a stool sits a hat that has clearly lost several arguments with moths. A rip near its brim opens, and it sings:

Oh, I was old when Dippet's chair still had a Dippet in it — I've sat on every thinking head and known it in a minute. The founders four each kept a door, and I was made the porter: the brave, the true, the sly, the wise — I sort, and never falter. So try me on, I'll tell no lies, though some I keep uneaten: there's things beneath this castle, dears, that never have been beaten. Put me on and don't be scared — of all the heads I've sat on, the ones that changed the world all shared one thing: they kept their hat on.

Idris, under his breath: "Did anyone else catch the third verse?" Take 1 Clue if you tell him yes.

The Sorting of You

Answer honestly, tallying G, S, R, H — the Hat is in your head and cannot be fooled, only argued with:

  1. The brass weight in your pocket is dangerous. Your first instinct that night was to — tell someone (H) · find out what it is yourself (R) · keep it close and see who comes for it (S) · you didn't have a first instinct, you had a first thrill (G)
  2. A staircase moves while you're on it. You — ride it and see (G) · note the pattern (R) · check no one small got left behind (H) · consider how this could be useful (S)
  3. Posy is about to be laughed at for something she said. You — laugh with her, loudly, first (H) · change the subject with something smarter (R) · stare down the laughers (G) · remember exactly who laughed (S)
  4. Which door? The one marked — locked (G) · library (R) · kitchens (H) · private (S)
  5. You'd rather be — right (R) · first (G) · essential (S) · missed (H)
  6. The Hat whispers, "I could make you great, you know." You answer — "Define great" (R) · "Go on, then" (S) · "Make me useful" (H) · "Make it interesting" (G)
  7. Your calamities are usually caused by — hurrying (G) · overthinking (R) · trusting (H) · wanting (S)
  8. Sixty years from now, the school should remember your — story (G) · answer (R) · kindness (H) · name (S)

Highest tally is the Hat's decision. On a tie, it lets you choose — and tells you, smugly, that it always meant to. Then it shouts your House to the hall, and one table in particular rises roaring for you.

Scene 3 · The Feast

Headmaster Armando Dippet rises — thin, ancient, wispy-haired, kindly in the way of a man who has been kindly so long it has worn grooves.

Dippet: "Welcome, welcome. Two notices. The Forbidden Forest is forbidden — the clue, as ever, is in the name. And the library's Restricted Section is closed to first-years absolutely; Madam Whisp has my full authority and, I am told, a new method." A pause. "Eat."

The gold plates fill themselves. The ghosts arrive through the walls to gasps — the Fat Friar beaming over the Hufflepuffs, Nearly Headless Nick performing his neck for the new Gryffindors, the Bloody Baron silencing a whole quarter of the hall, and the Grey Lady drifting the length of the Ravenclaw table, looking at no one. Insight, DC 12: she is looking at no one because she is looking at the doors of the hall — the ones toward the library. Take 1 Clue.

First night in the Tower. Prefect Odette Marsh (one shoe) herds the Gryffindor first-years up seven floors, teaches you the password at the Fat Lady's portrait — "Flibbertigibbet, and say it like you mean it, she docks marks for mumbling" — and the common room opens up all firelight and squashed armchairs and a hundred years of initials carved where the prefects pretend not to see. Your dormitory is at the top of the spiral stair: five four-posters, five trunks, a window with the whole black lake in it. Meet your dorm-mates and name them — the book suggests a quiet one who talks in his sleep in Latin, a loud one already homesick and hiding it badly, and one who unpacks seventeen jars of something unlabelled and says only "for later." Decide who gets which bed; it matters more than any of you will admit for seven years. If you brought an owl, it arrives at the window at midnight, judges the arrangements, and approves the sill.

That night, in a four-poster with the curtains drawn, the brass weight on your nightstand is warm for the first time. As if it's home. Chapter III ends. Refresh Luck. You may take 1 Downtime activity during your first days — then Chapter IV begins.

Chapter IV

Lessons & Whispers

September teaches you the castle the hard way: a hundred and forty-two staircases with opinions, doors that are walls on Thursdays, and the smell of toast that leads you — if you trust it — to wherever you were supposed to be.

Scene 1 · The Week of First Times

Play each first lesson as its own small scene. Every success below is +1 House Point; every calamity in class costs one, plus whatever the Miscast table thinks is funny.

  • Charms, with tiny, delighted Professor Plume, who stands on her desk to be seen and once, mid-sentence, on the air. Learn Lumos/Nox and Wingardium Leviosa. Cast the Levitation (DC 10) on your feather. Posy's feather, at the next desk, catches fire in a way the spell cannot explain. Peeves is later blamed. It was Peeves.
  • Transfiguration, with Professor Dumbledore, who turns his desk into a sow and back to make the point that the desk is the easy half. Match to needle: Wits, DC 12. Whatever happens, he finds something true to praise in it; on a calamity your match becomes a very small silver fish and he awards you a point for originality while flipping it kindly into a conjured bowl.
  • Potions, in the dungeons with Professor Slughorn — young for a master, waistcoated, collecting promising students the way Idris collects maps. Brew the Cure for Boils (see Cauldron & Ladle). Brew a clean batch and he says "Oho!" and remembers your surname; take the Mark ☐ Slughorn's Eye.
  • Defence Against the Dark Arts, with Professor Merrythought, iron-grey and unhurried, who has taught this subject longer than your parents have been alive. Learn Vermillious. She ends the first lesson: "The commonest dark thing you will meet is a lie that flatters you. Homework: twelve inches on a time you believed one."
  • Herbology, greenhouses, with Professor Beery, who weeps openly when the Puffapods bloom and casts every lesson like theatre. Insight, DC 10 to handle your Puffapod gently enough; flourish: it blooms in your hands and Beery declaims Shakespeare at you.
  • Astronomy, midnight, the tallest tower. Half the class falls asleep. If you stay awake (Nerve, DC 10): the grounds from above — and a light, once, deep in the Forbidden Forest, answering a light in the castle's library windows. Blink and both are gone. Take 1 Clue.
  • Flying, with Madam Wick, on school brooms that remember the 1890s. "Stick out your right hand and say UP like you mean it." Nerve, DC 10 (Broom-Sense applies). Triumph: your broom is the first up in the whole class, and across the lawn Lucinda Rooke forgets, for two seconds, not to look impressed.

Scene 2 · The Library, and Madam Whisp

You meet the library properly in your second week: a cathedral of shelves, ladders on brass rails, the Restricted Section sleeping behind a velvet rope that is definitely more than velvet. At the desk, small and straight-backed, sits Madam Elowen Whisp, who can hear a whisper at forty yards and a folded page corner at eighty.

The moment you cross the threshold, the brass weight in your pocket goes cold enough to feel through cloth. Cold, and — eager.

Madam Whisp, not looking up: "First-year. Three rules. Books return in the state they left. The Restricted Section is not a dare. And if ever you hear pages turning in a place with no books — you come and tell me, and no one else. Now. What do you need?"

Insight, DC 14. Success: her third rule wasn't library discipline. It was the voice of someone keeping a border. Take 1 Clue.

Chapter V

The Feast of All Hallows

By the last week of October the castle smells of pumpkin and cold stone, and a thousand live bats have moved into the Great Hall's rafters on, apparently, purpose.

Scene 1 · The Empty Chair

At the Halloween feast, between the third dessert and the ghost pantomime, you notice what nobody else has: Aurelia Sedge's seat is empty. The Slytherin prefect who reads at meals is not at the meal. If you took the Mark ☐ Rooke Knows Your Name, it is Lucinda who notices you noticing, and slides along the bench uninvited.

Rooke, quietly: "Sedge missed rounds last night too. And Tuesday. She's my house's prefect, so this is my business, and you keep being around when odd things happen, so apparently you're my business as well. Don't look flattered."

Scene 2 · Found

She is found before the feast ends — by you, if you go looking (leave the feast with whoever you choose; Posy comes without being asked, Idris brings a map, Rooke brings Rooke). The corridor outside the library is grey and still. Aurelia Sedge sits on the floor against the locked doors, awake, unhurt, holding an open book that is blank on every page — and she is looking at it fondly, turning the empty leaves one by one.

Sedge: "Shh. I'm nearly at the good part."

She does not know the month. She does not know her brother's name — she knows she has one, the way you know a word you've lost. Madam Bracken is sent for; so, oddly, is Madam Whisp, who arrives first, at a dead run, and stops herself from running the last ten feet so as not to frighten anyone. She looks at the blank book without touching it, and her face closes like a door.

Whisp: "Hospital Wing. She'll be — she'll be as well as care can make her." And then, so quietly that only someone standing exactly where you are standing could hear: "It's feeding again."

Take 1 Clue. If you own a sneakoscope, it is silent through all of this — no one here is lying — which is its own kind of information.

Scene 3 · What Peeves Saw

The one witness in that corridor is currently unscrewing the chandelier. Peeves knows something; Peeves always knows something; Peeves is Peeves. Pick your approach:

  • Bribe him with chaos — offer him your Gambol & Japes bag of tricks or an equally splendid disgrace (Charm, DC 12, Edge with the bag).
  • Trade insult for insult until he respects you (Nerve, DC 14 — he escalates).
  • Get the Bloody Baron's shadow to fall on him — only possible for a Slytherin, or with Rooke beside you and ☐ Saw Rooke Clearly.

Success, sung to the tune of a rude ballad while he loops through the ceiling: "The grey wet lady begged the door, the door said no-no-NO — the booky-book beneath the floor wants MORE MORE MORE — and a man with jackdaw buttons came a-knocking at the gate, but old Whisp-Whisp sent him packing! Peevesy saw! Peevesy ate his hat!" Take 2 Clues: the Grey Lady has tried to get in; a stranger with jackdaw buttons has tried the castle gates.

Scene 4 · The Grey Lady

Finding her takes three cold evenings (or one, with Idris's maps — he marks every place she is seen not to be, and triangulates). She stands at the end of a west-wing gallery, translucent against the window, and does not leave when you approach, which those who know her say is the most she has offered anyone in a century.

The Grey Lady: "You carry his key. I can hear it wanting." She turns; her eyes are the grey of the lake before rain. "The book under the library was bound long ago, by a clever man who thought memory could be collected, like beetles. Like things. My mother's people knew better: knowledge hoarded is knowledge starved, and a starved thing bites. The librarians have kept it sleeping for four hundred years. It sleeps badly now. Someone outside these walls is singing to it." A long pause. "You will want to know why I care." Another. "I know what it is to be diminished, page by page."

Take 2 Clues. If you ask her nothing at all — simply thank her — take the Mark ☐ The Lady's Regard: she will come when it matters.

Take 2 Downtime activities. This chapter's Investigate: find who wears jackdaw buttons (Charm or Wits, DC 12 — hospital-wing gossip, owl-post records, Slughorn's encyclopaedic memory for tailoring). Success: a name from Slughorn, delivered with uncharacteristic quiet — "Jackdaw buttons? That's Caradoc Burke, dear boy. Collector. The family disowned even him, which given the family is saying something. Do stay away." Take 1 Clue and the Mark ☐ The Name Burke.

Chapter VI

The Long Dark of Winter

Snow closes over the grounds like a lid. The lake freezes from the edges in; the Giant Squid keeps one polite hole open. In the common rooms, the fires grow opinions.

Scene 1 · The Letter with No Owl

A week into December a letter is simply in your bag, between Charms notes, on paper so good it feels like cloth. No owl brought it. The hand is elegant, the ink jackdaw-black:

"To the student in possession of a certain brass trifle — my compliments on your discretion. You hold a family heirloom, lost these many years. I will pay fifty galleons for its return, no questions in either direction. Leave it any Hogsmeade weekend at the Owl Office, box 9. Do not trouble the staff with this; they are sentimental about the cellar, and sentiment, at your age, is catching. — A Collector"

Decide what you do — this is the chapter's spine, and there is no wrong door, only different years:

  • Tell Madam Whisp. She reads it twice, thanks you with terrifying formality, and teaches you something "off the syllabus" — learn Auditio Minima a chapter early, from her rather than Idris. Take ☐ Whisp's Trust and 1 Clue.
  • Answer it — string Burke along, forge delays, learn what you can (Wits, DC 14, each Hogsmeade weekend; each success 1 Clue; a calamity means he learns something true about you — take ☐ Burke Knows Your Face).
  • Say nothing, keep the weight, keep watch. Solitary and brave and a little foolish: the Chapter IX vault scene will be harder, but take ☐ Nobody's Pawn — in the finale, Burke has no lever on you at all.
  • Sell it. Fifty galleons is fifty galleons. If you truly do it, take the gold and the Mark ☐ Sold the Key — Chapter IX still comes for you; it just comes angrier, and you will spend the spring winning back the right to fix what you fed.

Scene 2 · Christmas

Stay, or go home — both are whole scenes; play the one you choose.

Staying: a dozen students, one long table, Dippet in a cracker crown, Dumbledore conducting the carols so that the suits of armour sing the descant. Ogg hauls in twelve trees and cries when the first-years decorate the twelfth. On Christmas Eve, Madam Whisp is seen carrying mulled mead into the library, and if you follow (Nerve, DC 10 — the castle at night is a different castle), you find her setting the cup by the Restricted Section rope and saying, to the floor, quite gently: "Merry Christmas, you old horror." Take 1 Clue — the keeping is a kindness too; the book was a person's life-work once, and Whisp knows its name.

Going home: the house is smaller than it was in August, and lovelier. Someone asks if you've made friends and you find the answer changes your whole face. On Boxing Day an owl arrives from Posy with a parcel of her mother's shortbread and a card that says "the castle is WRONG without you, come back immediately, also Idris says the weight's pull has changed direction which he says is INTERESTING in his interesting voice, which, WORRY." Take 1 Clue.

Presents (either way): from Posy, a hand-labelled album for Chocolate Frog cards with three rare ones already in it. From Idris, a small map of the library corridor — annotated, in his tiny capitals: THE FLOOR IS OLDER THAN THE ROOM. ASK ME WHY. From your family, decide. If any Bond is 3, that friend's gift understands you almost frighteningly well; describe it.

Scene 3 · The Floor Is Older Than the Room

January. Idris spreads five maps of different centuries across a study table and stacks his reasoning like bricks: the library was rebuilt in the 1500s, two floors up from where the founders' library stood. The old reading room is still down there, sealed, in the dark — and every librarian since has held its key, passed hand to hand like a hot coal.

Idris: "Your brass weight isn't the key to the room. It's the key to the book. Burke got it out somehow, years ago — got caught, I'd bet, before he could come back for the rest. You can't steal a book like that in one go. You have to be invited back. It's been waiting for someone to carry its key through the front gates." He looks at you with terrible mildness. "It invited you."

Take 2 Clues. Take 3 Downtime activities for the deep winter. Investigate entry: what does the Ledger actually do with what it takes (Wits DC 14 in the open stacks, or Nerve DC 12 to interview the Grey Lady again; success: 1 Clue, and learn the word restitution — what is read aloud to it, it can be made to give back).

Chapter VII

The Thaw

The lake groans and cracks like a giant turning over. Quidditch resumes to universal hysteria. And you are, one way or another, in trouble.

Scene 1 · Detention

However careful you've been, some thread of the winter's rule-bending catches up — choose which (out after hours; a corridor "borrowed" restricted book; the vinegar barrel incident escalating). Sentence: detention, with Ogg, in the Forbidden Forest, after dark, because the 1930s had a theory of child development that mostly involved lanterns.

Ogg: "Stay by me, stay on the path, an' if somethin' white an' beautiful tells yeh to leave the path — 'specially then — yeh don't." He hands you a lantern the size of a birdcage. "Unicorn foal's gone strayin'. We fetch it home 'fore the cold does."

Run it as three checks, your choice of approach for each (track the foal: Insight; move quiet: Nerve; win its trust: Charm — DC 12 each; a companion on detention with you, if any Bond ≥ 2, gives Edge on one). Two successes: the foal is walked home glowing between you, and Ogg tells the story at breakfast so loudly you gain +3 House Points. On any calamity: the trees to your left, far off, hold two red points of light, watching — and between the trunks, briefly, a man's silhouette with bright buttons. It does not approach the lantern. Take 1 Clue: Burke cannot come closer. Something still bars him. He needs it carried out — or the barrier broken from inside.

Scene 2 · The Match

The spring Quidditch final (your house or not, everyone attends; it is not legally optional). Wear the scarf, learn the songs. During the match, choose one stand to sit in and one small scene to play: with Posy (she has invented a statistics notation for Quidditch and is inventing the field of sports analytics sixty years early); with Idris (he watches the crowd, not the game — "Whisp isn't here. Whisp is never not here"); with Rooke (she explains, coldly and precisely, why she'd be a better Seeker than the Seeker, and is right). If you sit with Idris: the library is unguarded during matches. Take 1 Clue, and file that away for Chapter IX.

Scene 3 · Sedge, Again

Aurelia Sedge came back from the Hospital Wing in November with most of her memories restored and all of her certainty gone. You've seen her since — smiling too carefully, carrying books she never opens now. In April she stops you, of all people, in an empty stairwell.

Sedge: "You're the one who found me. I remember that much — I remember being found." Her hands are steady; prefects' hands learn to be. "Whatever took the rest of it — I've started hearing it again. At night. Turning its pages under the floor, contented, like a cat by a fire. It's full, and it's still hungry. That's not how full works." She meets your eyes. "You're eleven. I should be telling a teacher. I've tried — Whisp thanks me and the walls go up. So I'm telling the person who came looking."

Take 1 Clue. Sedge is now an ally: in Chapter IX, if you want a prefect's authority, her hand on a door, or someone tall between you and Caradoc Burke for thirty seconds, she will be there. Take the Mark ☐ Sedge Remembers.

Take 3 Downtime activities. Investigate: assemble everything into a plan (Wits DC 12, Edge if your Clues already number 10+; success: 1 Clue and you may sketch, with Idris, an actual floor plan of the descent — keep it, it grants Edge on one check in Chapter IX).

Chapter VIII

Examinations

June arrives smelling of cut grass and panic. The Great Hall fills with single desks in rows, which is somehow the most frightening magic the castle has performed all year.

The Week of Reckoning

Exams use different dice — the Steady Quill rule: sit each exam as one roll of 2d10 + the named stat + that class's Study score (two ten-siders summed; no d10s? roll the d20 twice, halving each, rounded up). DC 12 to pass, 17 for Outstanding (each Outstanding: +2 House Points and one professor's open delight — play it). A double ten is a triumph; a double one means you wrote your name wrong and it gets read out. Why different dice? Because an exam hall is no place for heroics: two dice cluster toward the middle, so preparation, not luck, decides your year. The swingy d20 stays for everything brave, foolish and quick.

It is. It genuinely is.

The night the last exam ends, the common rooms are golden and loud. And the brass weight in your pocket turns so cold it burns, and from under the floors of the castle — clearly, unmistakably, in the bones of your ears — comes the sound of a great book closing. Take 1 Clue. It has finished waiting. Chapter VIII ends. Refresh Luck; you may take one last Rest, free.

Chapter IX

The Hollow Ledger

The last Saturday of term. Half the school is at the lake; the staff are at a leaving-feast rehearsal; the library, for one hour, is a held breath. If you don't go down tonight, no one ever will — no one but the man at the gates with jackdaw buttons, who has waited eleven years and can wait one more night. You can't. You've done the reading.

Count your Clues now. 0–6: the dark is ahead of you — all vault DCs below are +2. 7–11: you're ready — DCs as written. 12+: you walk down almost calmly — take Edge on any two checks of your choice below. Choose your company: any friends with Bond ≥ 2 may come; each may grant you Edge once tonight, but each is also someone the Ledger can reach for.

Scene 1 · Down

Three ways under the library; pick one.

  • The librarian's stair — through the Restricted Section rope while Whisp is at the rehearsal. Alohomora at DC 16 (the lock notices); with ☐ Whisp's Trust, don't roll — the rope simply lets you pass, and you understand, with a chill, that she has left it open for you. She knew. She has always known. She just couldn't be the one to do it; keepers keep.
  • Idris's route — a maintenance stair from the old kitchens, mapped in tiny capitals. Nerve, DC 12, in the pitch dark (Lumos first: casting in true darkness is Nerve — you remember).
  • Sedge's key — with ☐ Sedge Remembers: a prefect's authority walks you past every portrait, and Sedge herself stops at the final door. "This is as far as I go," she says, steady. "If I hear it again I don't think I'd come back. Shout, and I'll come anyway."

Scene 2 · The Reading Room

The founders' library is one room, low and vaulted, and it is not dark: candles that have burned for four hundred years stand in their own wax like frozen fountains. Empty shelves. One lectern. On the lectern, chained in silver, a book the size of a tombstone, bound in leather gone grey as the Grey Lady's gown — and it is reading you; you can feel the pages of yourself riffling. The brass weight rises gently from your pocket and hangs in the air, humming its one note, home.

The Ledger speaks the way a paper-cut stings — after the fact, softly:

The Ledger: You brought my key. Good child. Clever child. I have so much to give — every secret ever whispered in this castle is pressed in me like flowers. Ask. Ask anything. The first page is free.

The temptation is real and you should honestly consider it. One free question, answered truly — anything (what H.B.'s last note would have said; who you'll become; what the Hat kept quiet). If you take the page: the answer is true, and useful, and take the Mark ☐ Read the First Page — and the Ledger has your bookmark now; somewhere far off, one small memory of yours (you choose which — choose honestly) goes thin as tissue.

Then the far stair sounds with unhurried footsteps, and a voice you have only read arrives in the candlelight — a lean man gone soft at the edges, jackdaw buttons bright as eyes. However he got in, it cost him: frost on his shoulders in June, and one hand held wrong.

Caradoc Burke: "Eleven years I've stood at fences, child. Do you know what that book is? Every debt, every deed, every secret in wizard Britain, four centuries deep. The family threw me out for wanting to sell it — as if Borgin wouldn't have. As if anyone wouldn't have." He extends the good hand, reasonable as rain. "The key, now. You've carried it beautifully. I'll even pay the fifty galleons. You can be the richest first-year in the history of the school, or a footnote in a very sad story about a cellar. Old Burke's a fair man. Choose."

Scene 3 · The Choice of Endings

Play it however you and your dice decide — duel him (Form 14, Hits 3; his casting hand is ruined: after every exchange he loses, his Form drops by 1); trick him (the Forgetfulness Potion, if you brewed one this term, can be the whole plan — Wits DC 14 to deliver it, and ten minutes later he is a mild, puzzled man who cannot remember why he's in a cellar); stall him until help comes (with ☐ Sedge Remembers or ☐ Whisp's Trust or ☐ The Lady's Regard, help is one shout away — the Grey Lady rises through the lectern, and Burke's nerve, eleven years thin, breaks). If ever you're at 0 Stamina, you wake in the Hospital Wing to Ending IV — the adults finish it, and you hear it secondhand, which stings worse than anything Burke cast.

Then: the Ledger itself. The weight hangs waiting. Choose, and read your ending:

  • Restitution. (Requires the word — Chapter VI.) Set the key against the cover, and instead of opening it, read aloud to it — anything; Posy reads the shortbread card; you read your Hogwarts letter, Dear student, we are pleased—. Magic, DC 14, Edge if the Grey Lady attends. Success: the Ledger, fed a memory given freely for the first time in four centuries, sighs like a tide going out — and gives back. Sedge's missing pieces. The Grey Lady's stolen century of small joys. A margin-writer's unfinished sentence, delivered by owl, sixty years late, to a very old woman named Hesper who reads it and laughs. The book sleeps — properly, deeply. Whisp finds you on the stair, looks at you a long moment, and says only, "Shelved, I trust." The best ending, and the hardest earned.
  • Seal it. Snap the brass weight in half (Nerve, DC 12 — it screams up your arm the whole story of its making). The Ledger slams shut forever, keeping all it holds. Safe, and silent, and no one gets anything back. Whisp inherits a quieter vigil, and thanks you, and means it, and something in her face grieves anyway.
  • Surrender it to Dippet. The grown-up ending. The Headmaster listens to the whole year without interrupting once, says "Great heavens" four times, and has the room bricked over by August. You receive an award for services to the school, polite disbelief from most of it, and, at the leaving feast, from the staff table, Dumbledore raising his goblet a quarter-inch to you alone.
  • Keep the key. Walk away with the weight in your pocket and the book unfed behind you. The Ledger will wait — it's good at it — and somewhere in your third or fourth year, this story will come looking for its second chapter. (This is the sequel-hook ending. The Hat did warn you about keeping things uneaten.)

Scene 4 · The Leaving Feast

The Great Hall in June, banners in the rafters — count your House Points against your housemates' year: under 10, you were a quiet stitch in a big tapestry, and the tapestry needed you; 10–19, your name got cheered at least once and you know exactly which night; 20+, first-years are pointing you out to their parents on the platform. The House Cup goes where your tally and your heart say it goes — you were there; you know who earned it.

On the train south, the compartment smells of grass and toffee. Posy is asleep on a stack of every card you traded her. Idris is drawing next year's map, which includes, you notice, your house. Somewhere up the corridor Rooke walks past, pauses, and — where absolutely no one can see — waves.

The barrier at King's Cross lets you out into a London that doesn't know. You know. Nine weeks, and the scarlet engine breathes again.

The end of your first year.

Playing on: keep your sheet. Second year deserves: the sequel Mark you may have taken, whatever Bond reached 3, and one question you never got answered. The Oracle, as ever, is wearing the Hat.
