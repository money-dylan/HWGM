# When The Hollow Ledger Calls For Dice

Written 2026-08-27 after an audit: **3 rolls in 83 story turns — one every 28 —
and all three were Nerve.** Magic 3 (his best stat), Wits, Charm, Insight and
Luck had never been tested once. The fault was not "too few dice" as a quota; it
was that uncertain moments were being resolved by narration when they belonged
to the player and the die.

The player's instruction: *"I don't wanna roll unnecessarily, but if it makes
sense."* So this file defines **when it makes sense**, as conditions to check —
not a rate to hit.

---

## The three conditions

Call for a roll when **all three** are true. If any one fails, narrate instead.

1. **The outcome is genuinely uncertain.** Not settled by who Remus is, what he
   carries, or what the fiction has already established.
2. **Failure changes the story.** It costs something, complicates something, or
   sends the scene somewhere new. If failure just means "try again" or "you
   don't learn the thing the plot requires," there is no roll.
3. **He has committed to an action.** He has said what he is doing. Never roll
   to decide atmosphere, what an NPC feels, or what happens next in general.

When all three hold, **offer the roll rather than writing the outcome** — that
is the whole of the correction.

## Never roll for

- Anything a **talent already guarantees.** The Castle's ♥1 means he is never
  lost going to meals or lessons; Idris's ♥1 means never lost on a mapped floor.
  Rolling those insults the bond he earned.
- **Conversation that should simply be played.** Talking to friends is not a
  Charm check. Dice enter a social scene only when he attempts something
  specific and difficult on someone with their own agenda — persuading a
  prefect to look away, lying to a professor, getting a straight answer from a
  ghost who has refused one for four centuries.
- **Information the story must deliver.** Gate the *manner* of learning it,
  never the fact.
- **Ordinary school life.** Walking, eating, sitting, arriving.
- Anything where **he cannot lose anything he cares about.**

## Reach for the dice at these moments

These recur constantly and have been narrated past. Each is a legitimate check:

| Situation | Stat | Typical DC |
|---|---|---|
| First attempt at a new spell in a lesson | Magic | 8–12 |
| Casting under pressure, watched, or hurried | Magic | 12–16 |
| Recalling something read, or working out a rule | Wits | 8–12 |
| Noticing the detail he wasn't meant to notice | Insight | 12 |
| Reading whether someone is lying or holding back | Insight | 12–16 |
| Talking a person into something against their interest | Charm | 12–16 |
| Holding his nerve when the castle turns cold | Nerve | 12 |
| Anything physical and risky — climbing, running, catching | Nerve | 8–12 |
| Handling the weight when it wants something | Nerve | 14+ |
| Flying, duelling, brewing under time | varies | 12–16 |

**Rotate the stats.** If three consecutive checks used the same stat, the fourth
almost certainly should not. His sheet has six; a term that only tests Nerve is
a term where five sixths of his character never mattered.

## The DC ladder (as printed in his rulebook)

**8** simple · **12** tricky · **16** hard · **20** the stuff of school legend.
Beat it by 5 for a *flourish* (a small extra benefit the PLAYER chooses),
which is **spent in the moment it is earned** — see below. Natural 20 is a
triumph, +1 House Point if witnessed. Natural 1 is a calamity — if casting, the
Table of Miscasts.

## Edge and Burden

Now supported: `--ask "label|Stat|mod|dc|edge"` (or `burden`). The table rolls
two dice and keeps the better or the worse, shows both, and says which.

Grant **Edge** for real advantages in the fiction: a friend helping, the right
tool, genuine preparation, a talent that fits sideways. Grant **Burden** for
darkness, fear, injury, sabotage, an audience he is afraid of. They cancel one
for one. Naming the reason aloud in the prompt label is part of the reward —
*"Wingardium Leviosa, with Nell steadying the desk — Edge."*

## Fail forward, always

A failed roll never ends a scene or blocks the story. It costs: House Points,
time, a scene witnessed by the wrong person, a friend's confidence, a
complication that arrives later. Calamities are opportunities — the miscast that
makes the lesson memorable, the overheard denial that becomes a thread.

## He may ask

If he says "can I try X?" and the three conditions hold, that is a roll — set it
up rather than narrating around it. His very first request for this game was
wanting to roll a real d20 at his desk and enter the number. Honour that.

## The check before filing a turn

> Did anything in this turn *actually* hang in the balance — and did I resolve
> it myself instead of handing him the dice?

## Scripted checks vs the three conditions

Added after the grey cloak in Maisie's Chapter I, where the book scripts
**Insight DC 10** to notice the man watching the shop doorways, and the GM
instead wrote her low Insight as narration — *"he is an obstacle and you treat
him as one"* — and told the player afterwards. That decided an outcome that
belonged to the player, which is the precise fault this file exists to correct.

**The rule:**

1. **When the book scripts a check, offer it.** As a button, never a demand — he
   may ignore it. Scripted perception beats carry Marks and Clues; those are his
   to win or miss, and a stat of -1 is honestly played by rolling it and
   failing, not by narrating around it.
2. **The three conditions still gate checks the GM invents.** Uncertain outcome,
   failure that costs, an action he has committed to. Nothing changes there.
3. **Never resolve by narration anything with a Mark or a Clue attached.** If
   something is at stake on the page, the die decides it.
4. **Passive noticing with nothing at stake stays narration.** Atmosphere, an
   NPC's mood, what a room feels like — no dice, as before.

The commitment clause is about not rolling for atmosphere and not rolling for
what an NPC feels. It was never a licence to pre-empt a scored beat.

## Flourishes are spent in the moment — 2026-08-29, player ruling, BOTH SLOTS

Dylan ruled this at Maisie's table and asked for it across every campaign. It
replaces the banking habit both slots had drifted into.

- **A flourish is spent on the turn it is earned.** Name the benefit in the
  same turn, in the fiction. Never carry one forward; never keep a running
  total of unspent ones.
- **The choice belongs to the player**, per the rulebook: *"take a small extra
  benefit (you choose, within reason)."* Offer a concrete one so play does not
  stall, and let them name their own instead whenever they would rather.
- **It may be a lasting asset.** The rulebook's own model is the Gringotts
  cart: *"Edge on your next Charm check with any goblin, ever."* A Mark is a
  good shape for one.
- **All previously banked flourishes are retired**, in both slots — Maisie's
  four (Ch I ×2, the trunk, Rooke) and Remus's two (the matchstick, the Lumos).
  That is the price of the rule and is not owed back.
- The player-facing rulebook (`hollow-ledger.html`, "Reading the Die") already
  said the right thing and needs no change: the drift was the GMs', not the
  book's.
